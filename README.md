# Ransomware Risk Score (for Websites)

We want to build a **single risk score** for each website that says how likely it is to be hit by ransomware.

We start from what’s already happened: we collect a list of sites that were attacked in the past (source: [ransomware.live](https://ransomware.live)). For each of those sites, we use **Wappalyzer** to fingerprint the technologies in use—things like CMSs, frameworks, servers, and plugins. Then we look for other websites with **similar technology stacks** and use that similarity to compute a first, simple risk score: the more a site’s tech profile resembles known victims, the higher its score.

## "Unique Domains":  
We take a random sample of **3,041,353** domains from the **Spiceworks** database (year **2022**), covering **USA**, **Canada**, and **APAC**. We fingerprint these sites with Wappalyzer as well and compare their stacks to those of the known victims. This lets us **flag** websites that “look like” past targets and might therefore be more attractive to attackers.

Our goal is to **monitor future ransomware incidents** to see whether the flagged sites actually get hit. Those observations help us validate the approach and **refine** the scoring over time.

# PROBLEM STATEMENT
A really HUGE Problem with this databases is merging them together. There might be the same attack reported in multiple databases, but with different organization names. For example, the attack on the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF) is reported in both the ransomware.live and the Maryland database, but with different names: "University of California, San Francisco" vs "University of California San Francisco", or IKEA Kuwait vs IKEA Marocco. This makes it difficult to merge the databases and analyze the data. I need help with merging the databases together and identifying the same attacks across different databases. There might be some LLM-based approaches to identify the same attacks across different databases.
